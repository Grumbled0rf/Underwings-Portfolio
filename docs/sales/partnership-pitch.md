# Audit-Firm & Referral Partnership Kit

> **Goal:** build a referral channel — partners who send Underwings their
> clients' remediation / implementation / testing work.
> **Why this is the highest-conversion channel:** a referral arrives with
> the partner's trust already attached. You're not cold; you're
> "recommended by someone they pay for advice."
> **The core insight:** auditors and certification bodies *find* problems
> but are often barred — by independence rules — from *fixing* them. They
> need delivery partners to refer to. Underwings wants to be that partner.

---

## 1. Why this works (the independence angle)

The strongest version of this isn't "please send us work." It's "you have a
gap, and we fill it."

- **ISO certification bodies / registrars** legally cannot consult on the
  ISMS they certify — that would destroy their independence. When a client
  fails or isn't ready, the body can't fix it. They keep informal lists of
  implementation partners to refer to. **You want to be on those lists.**
- **Financial / statutory audit firms** increasingly get asked "can you also
  help with our cyber / ADHICS / PDPL?" — and most have no real answer.
  They either lose the client's goodwill or refer it out. Give them someone
  to refer to and you make them look complete.
- **IT MSPs and system integrators** run infrastructure but rarely have an
  OSCP-level tester or a Lead Auditor on staff. They get asked for pen tests
  and compliance and have to say no, or sub-contract badly.

In every case you are *complementary, not competitive*. That's the whole pitch.

## 2. Who to target — ranked by fit

| Rank | Partner type | Why they refer | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ISO certification bodies / registrars** operating in the UAE | Independence bars them from remediation; they refer readiness work out | Reliable implementation so their certified clients pass cleanly |
| 2 | **Boutique / mid-tier audit & accounting firms** | Clients ask for cyber/ADHICS/PDPL they can't deliver | A trustworthy specialist who won't poach the relationship |
| 3 | **Law firms doing UAE PDPL / data-protection advisory** | They advise on the law; clients need the technical + ISMS implementation | A partner who handles the technical side they don't |
| 4 | **IT MSPs / system integrators** | Asked for pen tests + compliance they can't staff | Senior testing + GRC they can white-label or co-deliver |
| 5 | **Management consultancies** (ops / healthcare / risk) | Cyber is adjacent to their work, not their core | A specialist to bring in on the security workstream |

**Healthcare angle:** any partner with healthcare clients in Abu Dhabi is
gold — ADHICS is mandatory, the need is non-discretionary, and you're the
specialist. Lead the ADHICS article into these conversations.

## 3. What you offer a partner (make it easy to say yes)

1. **You make them look good.** Their client gets senior, reliable, on-time
   delivery — named practitioners, not juniors. Their reputation is safe.
2. **You don't poach.** Put it in writing: you won't solicit the partner's
   client for services outside the referred scope, and the relationship
   stays theirs. This is the #1 fear; kill it explicitly.
3. **Referral commission** — 10–15% of the first engagement's value, paid on
   the client's first payment to you. Simple, written, no exclusivity.
4. **Reciprocal referrals** — when your clients need an audit, a
   certification body, legal PDPL advice, or managed IT, you send them back.
   A two-way street is far stickier than a one-way fee.
5. **Co-branded or white-label option** for MSPs who want to keep their brand
   in front of the client (deliver under their name, you're the engine room).

> Keep the commercial terms one page and signable. A 12-page MSA kills
> momentum. A one-page referral agreement gets signed.

---

## 4. The commercial model (keep it simple)

| Term | Default |
|---|---|
| Referral fee | 12% of first engagement value, paid within 14 days of the client's first payment to Underwings |
| Reciprocal | Underwings refers audit / certification / legal / MSP work back, no fee expected either way after the first deal |
| Exclusivity | None |
| No-poach | Underwings won't solicit the referred client beyond the referred scope without the partner's knowledge |
| Term | Rolling, either party can end with 30 days notice; in-flight referrals honoured |

Adjust the fee per partner type — for certification bodies a reciprocal-only
(no fee) arrangement is often cleaner and avoids any appearance of
inducement that could compromise their independence. **For certification
bodies, lead with reciprocal referrals, not commission.**

---

## 5. How to approach them

Same warm-first discipline as the client outreach:

1. **Warm partners first.** Anyone the three of you already know at an audit
   firm, a registrar, an MSP, a law firm. A coffee beats a cold email.
2. **Certification bodies you've worked with** as a Lead Auditor (Manoj) —
   you already have relationships in that world. Start there.
3. **Cold but specific** — UAE-registered ISO certification bodies, boutique
   audit firms with healthcare/SME client bases. Reference the independence
   gap explicitly.

The meeting goal is simple: get onto their "who do we refer remediation to?"
list, and agree to send each other one client to test the relationship.

## 6. Outreach templates

### 6a. Warm — to someone you know at an audit firm / registrar / MSP
> Hi [name] — I've started a UAE cybersecurity practice, Underwings (I'm an
> ISO 27001 Lead Auditor; we also do OSCP-level pen testing and ADHICS work).
> I think there's a clean fit with [their firm]: you [audit / certify /
> advise], and clients often need someone independent to actually *implement*
> the fixes — without you stepping outside your independence. We'd handle
> that delivery, make you look good, refer work back, and never touch the
> relationship beyond the scope you send us. Worth a quick coffee to see if
> it's useful?

### 6b. Cold — to a certification body / registrar
> Hi [name] — Underwings is a UAE cybersecurity & compliance practice led by
> a named ISO 27001 Lead Auditor. We know certification bodies can't consult
> on the ISMS you certify, so readiness and remediation get referred out. We
> would like to be a delivery partner you can confidently refer to — senior
> people, transparent AED pricing, clients who arrive at your audit actually
> ready. We refer certification work back, and we'd keep it reciprocal rather
> than commission-based to respect your independence. Open to a short call?

### 6c. Cold — to a boutique audit / accounting firm
> Hi [name] — quick idea. Your clients increasingly ask about cyber, ADHICS,
> and PDPL — work that sits outside statutory audit. Underwings (UAE,
> ISO 27001 Lead Auditor + OSCP) can be the specialist you refer that to:
> we deliver, you keep the relationship, we pay a referral fee and send work
> back. It makes your firm look complete without hiring a security team.
> Worth 20 minutes?

## 8. Tracking

Same sheet / Krayin discipline as client outreach, but tag these as
`partner` leads (lead source "Referral Partner"). A signed partner who
sends even one client per quarter is worth more than dozens of cold leads —
treat partner relationships as accounts to nurture, not one-off touches.

---

## 9. Honest expectation-setting

Partnerships are a *slow-burn, high-value* channel. A certification body
won't refer you a client the week you meet — they refer once they trust you,
which often means after they've seen you deliver one engagement well. So:

- Partnerships and direct founding-client outreach run **in parallel**.
- Your first founding client (from §direct outreach) is what *proves* you to
  partners. Land that first, then partnerships compound.
- Target: 2–3 signed referral relationships in the first quarter, each
  sending 1+ client over the following two quarters.
